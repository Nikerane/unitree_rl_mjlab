# Impact-speed dose pair: measurement and feasibility review (2026-09-22)

Scope: independent, read-only review of the completed `p=0.2`/`p=0.3` pair. I inspected the banked first-episode JSONL traces, current code and indexed provenance; I ran no simulation, learning, or source edits. The [pair result](../../results/2026-09-22_z1_speed_impulse_dose_pair.md) remains the authoritative campaign verdict: lower J6 impulse severity, unchanged over-cap frequency, no E1 qualification. This review does **not** assert that either limit is infeasible or propose changing a cap.

## Decisive observations

1. **The reported J6 velocity-limit frequency is entirely a startup phenomenon in this cohort.** Re-aggregating each arm's [control-step traces](../../results/assets/2026-09-22_speed_dose_pair/dose2/evaluation/trace.jsonl) ([`p=0.3`](../../results/assets/2026-09-22_speed_dose_pair/dose3/evaluation/trace.jsonl)) gives 384/384 episodes per arm with their *episode maximum* J6 substep speed at control step 0. At that step all 384 worlds per arm exceed 3.1415 rad/s, `started=false`, J6 contact-window impulse is zero, and impulse CaT delta is zero. The peak is identically 4.609762 rad/s across all goals/worlds for `p=0.2` and 4.580195 for `p=0.3`; no post-accepted-contact control interval exceeds the J6 velocity limit in either arm. The apparent 100% episode-level J6 velocity failure therefore cannot be attributed to impact or to failure of the impulse dose to reduce *contact-phase* velocity. It still is a genuine measured model-limit exceedance and a qualification blocker.
2. **Both learned first commands are saturated, in opposite J6 directions.** In all 384 first steps of `p=0.2`, raw J6 position action lies in `[-1.906,-1.543]`; in all 384 first steps of `p=0.3`, it lies in `[2.816,3.734]`. The evaluated wrapper uses `raw_policy_clip=1.0` ([saved contract](../../results/assets/2026-09-22_speed_dose_pair/dose2/evaluation/summary.json)), so effective J6 action is respectively −1 or +1. Both arms command `Kp6=800` on step 0, the lower endpoint of the unchanged exponential gain map ([mapping](../../../src/tasks/hammer/mdp/variable_impedance.py), [`C=1.25` config](../../../src/tasks/hammer/config/z1/__init__.py)). A representative post-step *signed* J6 velocity is +2.024 versus −2.121 rad/s; the peak-held absolute speeds are about 4.6. Opposite signs with similar magnitudes support a saturated initial-setpoint transient as the leading hypothesis, not an impulse-contact mechanism; they do not prove J6 decoupling from the other five joints.
3. **The reset pose does not by itself prescribe a J6 jump.** The fixed left-route start has J6=1.5544 rad ([pose table](../../../src/tasks/hammer/config/z1/__init__.py)); the reset callback explicitly writes zero joint velocity ([reset callback](../../../src/tasks/hammer/mdp/references.py)). This J6 angle matches the banked default offset 1.55439997 rad. The qualified absolute-default-offset position action has J6 scale 0.05516457 rad and a much wider physical clip, ±2.79253 rad ([contract](../../../src/tasks/hammer/config/z1/data/z1_joint_position_stage1.json), [action construction](../../../src/tasks/hammer/config/z1/env_cfgs.py); installed `mjlab.envs.mdp.actions.actions.JointPositionAction` applies scale + default offset before physical clipping). Thus the two clipped first actions request approximately 1.49924 and 1.60956 rad from a 1.5544-rad start. At zero initial velocity, `800 N·m/rad × 0.05516 rad ≈ 44.1 N·m` is an **inferred proportional component**, above the model's 30 N·m J6 effort value; it is not a measured actuator torque because damping, coupling and actuator saturation intervene. A zero J6 position coordinate would initially target the reset angle, so a safe first-step trajectory is plausible, not demonstrated.
4. **The impulse-cap question is different.** The experimental task inherits `[0.369,0.246,0.738,0.369,0.246,0.0164] N·m·s`, with J6's 0.0164 coming from a horizontal-route pilot constant reused by the diagonal/quintic task ([factory](../../../src/tasks/hammer/config/z1/__init__.py)); the saved evaluation contract confirms this exact vector and `subtract_baseline=true`, 25 substeps ([summary](../../results/assets/2026-09-22_speed_dose_pair/dose2/evaluation/summary.json)). This vector is **not** the banked `[1.64,3.28,1.64,…]`, the no-`2x` provisional `[0.82,1.64,0.82,…]`, or the uniform-0.9 diagnostic vector whose limited provenance is documented in [cap provenance](IMPULSE_CAP_PROVENANCE.md). The indexed material does not establish a manufacturer J6 reaction/damage limit or demonstrate that 0.0164 is attainable for this left-start, speed-conditioned useful strike. Within the pair the denominator is held fixed, so reduced J6 p95/maximum is a real reduction in the *measured* reaction-window quantity, but 128/128, 128/128 and 127/128 first episodes over cap at the three goals in **both** arms cannot establish cap compliance. Do not loosen the cap merely because it binds; first establish its scientific meaning and a controlled utility-versus-load feasibility envelope.

## What the sensors actually mean

- [SubstepPeakJointVel](../../../src/tasks/hammer/mdp/velocity_bound.py) samples `abs(robot.joint_vel)` each 2 ms physics substep and peak-holds per joint over the 10-substep/20 ms control interval; reset and the first substep clear the peak. The [evaluator observer](../../../.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-21-vega-speed-qualification/dose_eval.py) exports that exact buffer after native metrics but before auto-reset via [PreResetSnapshot](../../../scripts/evaluate_impact_speed.py). Hence the 4.6 rad/s step-0 readings are not merely post-decimation aliases or stale terminal/reset tensors. The control-rate signed velocity at step end is about ±2.0 in a sample world, demonstrating why a 50 Hz-only read would miss the earlier peak. The recorded `started=false` is the first-strike tracker's *accepted-contact latch*, not a direct every-substep contact trace; J6 reaction impulse zero provides independent supporting evidence of no accepted hammer–nail contact on step 0.
- [SubstepImpulseAccumulator](../../../src/tasks/hammer/mdp/impulse_bound.py) reads per-DoF `qfrc_constraint`, subtracts the frozen off-contact baseline, takes absolute value, gates by the hammer–nail contact sensor, and sums 25 substeps (50 ms) in a time-based sliding window. A control read returns the maximum rolling sum observed in that 20 ms interval; per-episode peak is retained. This is a **contact-gated windowed joint reaction** in N·m·s, not an instantaneous force, net object impulse, hardware damage value, or pure isolated impact torque. `qfrc_constraint` also includes other constraint components, so baseline subtraction/contact gating improve attribution without independently proving a physical reducer load. The separate object-side delivered impulse integrates axial contact force and is not interchangeable with Λ_j ([same module](../../../src/tasks/hammer/mdp/impulse_bound.py)).
- All 768 first episodes are successful, productive, finalized and non-timeout in the banked [episode outputs](../../results/assets/2026-09-22_speed_dose_pair/dose2/evaluation/episodes.jsonl) ([`p=0.3`](../../results/assets/2026-09-22_speed_dose_pair/dose3/evaluation/episodes.jsonl)). Median first accepted contact is step 10/10/9 for `p=0.2` and 9/8/8 for `p=0.3`; almost all J6 over-cap episodes are already violating at the success-terminal read (128/128, 128/128, 127/128 in both arms). The [tracker](../../../src/tasks/hammer/mdp/first_strike.py) finalizes on success or its event-window boundary; native evaluation auto-resets on task termination. These records do not establish contact release or a complete uncensored 50 ms post-onset history. Different contact/terminal timings can change how many 50 Hz reads see a rolling window; compare event peaks and native horizons, not read counts alone.

## Minimal no-learning probes to resolve the uncertainty

These are proposals only; none is authorized or run here. Keep source/checkpoint, reset state, RNG, all existing safety settings, fixed goal and physical start identical within each comparison, record **all 2 ms substeps** and the applied targets/torques rather than inferring them from raw action. Do not adopt an added hold step as the trained policy's fix on the strength of a diagnostic.

1. **Startup counterfactual (first priority):** same saved policy and first transition, compare its normal first command with a one-transition counterfactual that replaces only J6 position target by the *current/reset J6 angle*, keeping stiffness, other five actions and timing unchanged. Also inspect an all-joints-current-position first command only if J6-only replacement leaves the overshoot, to separate whole-arm coupling from reset/actuator settling. Record J6 substep peak and signed trajectory, native target, effort/constraint terms, head path and subsequent contact/hit/delivered impulse. A below-limit J6 peak in either same-duration counterfactual disproves inevitability of the startup excursion in this state; a persistent peak does not prove global infeasibility. Check whether the peak merely moves to the next control interval when normal commands resume.
2. **Independent goal-response diagnostic:** after an identical normal first motion and before accepted contact, override only the latched goal, recompute actor/critic observations, and continue paired low/mid/high rollouts with full safety telemetry. This isolates later goal sensitivity from the common first-step velocity transient. It is an unqualified diagnostic, **not** E1 promotion while the velocity/safety gate fails.
3. **If the cap remains binding after startup isolation:** with no learning, stratify pre-reset per-joint Λ, delivered object impulse, contact quality/normal, J6 applied target/gain and complete versus censored contact duration across matched traces; one carefully scoped physical/action intervention or a release-observed shadow can then test whether any useful strike under the current 0.0164 boundary is reachable. Specify the endpoint and preserve original terminal observations. A finite grid or failed policy sample cannot prove infeasibility; physical cap revision requires separate evidence and approval.

## Review position / disagreement to carry forward

The pair does **not** show that impact impulse causes the J6 velocity-limit failure: its 100% episode-level velocity exceedance is already incurred before contact on the first commanded step. Nor does it show the reaction cap is impossible, safe, or a certified hardware limit. The `p=0.3` severity decrease is valuable native-observed evidence, but unchanged threshold frequency and mixed other-joint/velocity tails prevent an overall safety claim. I would keep the existing failed E1 decision and decouple two questions before further learning or dose tuning: (a) can the first 20 ms be made velocity-compliant without moving the excursion or losing the strike, and (b) is useful speed-conditioned contact feasible under the current, separately justified J6 reaction-window boundary? A separately approved *unqualified* selector diagnostic may answer controllability, but it must not be labeled safety qualification or deployed as a compliant policy.
