# Speed-conditioned impulse CaT: mechanism review

2026-09-22. Independent source inspection; no implementation, simulation, training, or external-source claims. Scope is the fresh matched 0.2/0.3 pair, with the older 150-update audit used only as separately labelled mechanism evidence. The parent review independently audits raw timing. Source citations below are repository-relative with line numbers from this checkout.

## Bottom line

The strongest supported interpretation is **movement along a utility–severity trade-off without crossing the J6 boundary**, not a disconnected safety signal and not proof that the cap is infeasible. Another ceiling is a legitimate hypothesis test but is not yet the most informative next action. First distinguish insufficient terminal leverage, normalization effects, and a physically constrained utility frontier using existing evidence. No setting here guarantees a violation-frequency target.

The fresh pair has identical initial hashes and improves J6 p95/max severity at every goal, but leaves counts at 128/128/127. This does not imply an unresponsive learner: those counts are a thresholded statistic that stays saturated while a whole distribution moves downward above the boundary. Conversely, reduced severity cannot be relabelled compliance. Middle-goal delivered impulse retention is only 90.200%, so another increase has little demonstrated utility headroom under the frozen 90% guard. One policy instance per dose cannot establish a population dose-response law. [Result](../../results/2026-09-22_z1_speed_impulse_dose_pair.md)

## What the actual mechanism does

1. **Terminal violations are charged.** `CatPPO._cat_scale_reward` computes `r_positive*(1-delta)+r_negative`, without clipping; `process_env_step` applies it to every transition, including terminal transitions (`src/tasks/hammer/rl/cat_ppo.py:34–44,66–71`). Thus this is not the classic implementation mistake of discounting only the nonexistent future after success.
2. **True termination eliminates additional continuation leverage.** The return recursion uses `(1-delta)*(1-hard_done)` in both bootstrap and GAE recursion (`cat_ppo.py:86–103`). At success, continuation is already zero. If impulse violation occurs only at the last read, its immediate positive payout is attenuated once; earlier impulse/velocity hazards can still attenuate the return leading to it. It is wrong both to say terminal CaT has no effect and to presume it enjoys the repeated survival pressure of a long continuing task.
3. **The penalty is smooth in excess, not an explicit frequency objective.** With configured `min_p=0` (`src/tasks/hammer/config/z1/env_cfgs.py:497–512`), the map is `delta_j=p*clamp((Lambda_j-cap_j)/cmax_j,0,1)` for positive excess, otherwise zero (`src/tasks/hammer/cat/constraint_manager.py:48–57`). Infinitesimal exceedance incurs infinitesimal pressure. There is no fixed charge merely for crossing the boundary. Increasing `p` increases the slope and ceiling, not a hard chance constraint or a guarantee of zero overcap episodes.
4. **The active hazard is a maximum, not a sum.** `get_probs` takes the maximum across joints and constraint terms (`constraint_manager.py:76–80`). Improving an impulse margin below an already-active velocity hazard gives no further immediate CaT benefit until it changes that maximum. The older audit rules out velocity masking as the dominant explanation for those specific replay trajectories; the fresh pair must be checked separately. A global shared `min_p` change would also alter velocity behavior: it is not a clean impulse-only frequency intervention.
5. **Impulse normalization is population-history-dependent.** Per-joint scale is first seeded from a violating batch maximum, then updated by a violation-masked EMA, with a floor; it is retained through episode reset (`src/tasks/hammer/cat/hook.py:201–205,303–357`). Reducing all excesses can reduce the normalizer as well, partly preserving relative normalized severity. Batch outliers can weaken pressure on typical worlds. Neither possibility proves a fault. The code deliberately avoids idle decay and cross-joint scale contamination. A fresh 128-world evaluator is not the 1024-world training history.

## Why violating success can remain rational

For a simplified terminal comparison with no preceding hazards, let positive task payout be `P`, other costs `C>=0`, and terminal hazard `d`. The learner receives `(1-d)P-C`. A violating successful strike can beat a safe attempt whenever

`(1-d_unsafe)P_unsafe-C_unsafe > P_safe-C_safe`.

At ceiling 0.3, 70% of the positive terminal payout survives (provided impulse determines the total hazard). This is an incentive, not a veto. A safe attempt that fails to drive the nail, misses the requested speed, or loses enough delivered impulse can rationally be worse under this objective. Merely observing a positive unsafe terminal reward is **not sufficient** to prove that violations maximize expected return: safe counterfactual returns, trajectory duration, earlier costs/hazards, and attainable policies are missing.

The objective genuinely rewards useful impact: first-strike onset-speed payout is proportional to latched precontact speed (`src/tasks/hammer/mdp/rewards.py:414–434`); delivered payout can remain linear above its reference (`rewards.py:449–479`; event-linear configuration at `src/tasks/hammer/config/z1/env_cfgs.py:558–565`). The speed-goal term is a bounded squared error, charged once at finalization or failure, and explicitly survives CaT unscaled (`src/tasks/hammer/mdp/impact_speed.py:148–170,173–210`; `src/tasks/hammer/cat/hook.py:41–48`). Completion depends on depth, not safety (`src/tasks/hammer/mdp/rewards.py:114–127`; `src/tasks/hammer/mdp/terminations.py:19–29`). So safety competes with useful impact and success; it is not lexicographically prior to them.

The older audit reports 90.2–98.4% of positive reward on J6-violating terminal reads and positive post-CaT terminal rewards. That directly motivates this mechanism hypothesis, but those fractions belong to the older policies, not automatically to the fresh pair. ## Ranked hypotheses and discriminating predictions

1. **Finite terminal leverage + competing task payoff (most grounded).** Prediction: fresh pair traces show terminal concentration, and frozen-transition sensitivity demonstrates that modest ceiling changes remove little additional episode return relative to task payout. This is descriptive sensitivity, not a counterfactual learned-policy prediction.
2. **Safe utility frontier or exploration/access problem (important, unresolved).** Prediction: matched successful low-utilization trajectories exist only with poorer speed accuracy/impulse/tracking, or are absent from sampled trajectories. Absence from two learned policies does not prove physical impossibility. A single verified all-cap compliant, utility-preserving trajectory would demonstrate sampled feasibility; no such witness is established here.
3. **Relative normalization/saturation limits information (plausible).** Prediction: training snapshots show cmax moving with typical excess, many clipped values, or a large gap between typical and batch-maximum excess. The fresh evaluation probability reversal at the middle goal is insufficient evidence; learned trajectories and normalizers both differ.
4. **Late useless follow-through (lower priority).** Older terminal-inclusive audit found little growth after 95% of useful impulse had already arrived. That weakens the old simple explanation, without resolving within-20ms control-bin timing or post-success unobserved contact. First-strike data finalize at success or window end (`src/tasks/hammer/mdp/first_strike.py:353–367`); disabling termination would change both measurement and optimization, not simply expose an existing equivalent objective.

## Minimal next diagnostics, in order

1. **Existing fresh-pair trace accounting:** per episode align first contact, first cap crossing, first useful-delivery milestones, and success; report positive reward mass and learner reward on those reads, separate impulse/velocity winner, and event survival product `product(1-delta)` over the native horizon. Preserve the distinction between native observed and full-contact safety. No new training needed.
2. **Frozen-history sensitivity:** hold a trace's margins and its reconstructed evaluation normalizer sequence fixed; vary only impulse ceiling, preserve velocity max arbitration, and recompute immediate reward plus Monte Carlo discounted returns. Report where higher ceiling ceases to change the active hazard. This isolates mathematical leverage on those trajectories, not training causality, historical PPO advantages, or predicted behavior. Compare policies under one explicitly declared common reference scale as a separate diagnostic, never overwrite their native telemetry.
3. **Determine historical identifiability:** inventory saved training signals/state before claiming normalization caused the outcome. If normalizer snapshots and relevant rollout margins are absent, historical training pressure is not recoverable from final actor weights or fresh replay. If future training is approved, capture per-joint cmax, normalized-excess quantiles, clipped fraction, and event-relative hazard/payout, rather than another uninstrumented dose point.
4. **Feasibility witness before redesign:** inspect existing successful trajectories for best joint-cap utilization at matched requested/measured speeds and utility. Report a Pareto frontier, not only averages. An authorized later no-learning trajectory/impedance probe could seek a witness; it cannot be replaced by silently relaxing caps or declaring them impossible.

Only then choose a new dose or mechanism experiment. A dose increase keeps the mechanism fixed and is easiest to interpret, but the current evidence does not nominate an optimal next numeric value. Changing terminal credit, adding an impulse-specific violation floor, freezing/altering normalization, or using an explicit constraint-cost formulation are materially different interventions requiring separate design and authorization. Increasing completion reward alone is especially not a generic repair: it also increases the payout retained by unsafe success. No immediate source change or training launch is recommended by this review.
