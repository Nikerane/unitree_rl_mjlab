# Full-strike softer-wrist diagnostic: direction and primary literature

2026-09-22. Research note; no training or production gain recommendation.

## Direction assessment

The parent investigation reports that replaying identical startup commands with the original high wrist gains produces a peak near 4.6 rad/s at 2 ms physics but about 0.473 rad/s at 0.0625 ms; fixed wrist gains Kp=256, Kd=25.6 produce approximately 0.48 rad/s at 2 ms without pre-contact chatter. These are supplied local measurements, not independently rerun for this literature note. They support a strong timestep-sensitive startup-dynamics diagnosis. They do **not** demonstrate full-strike feasibility, hardware safety, or an advantage of learned variable impedance.

The proposed bounded full-strike closed-loop evaluation is the right next question: does the numerically calmer fixed-gain intervention preserve useful impact and task progress, or does it merely avoid moving hard enough? Keep the existing policy, initial conditions, nominal 2 ms physics/20 ms control, effort caps, rewards, and constraint definitions unchanged; record the modified wrist gains explicitly. This is a changed-dynamics diagnostic, not qualification of the untouched checkpoint. Unlike frozen-command replay, a closed-loop rollout changes subsequent observations and actions; its purpose is system-level performance, not isolation of identical commands.

Record paired outcomes for the original and softer wrist: completion/nail progress, contact timing, ante-impact axial speed, delivered impact, per-joint event impulse and constraint violation, position-tracking error, saturation fraction, and physics-step velocity/torque sign reversals. Separate pre-contact startup, impact, and post-impact windows. Lower peak velocity without comparable task accomplishment is not a safety-performance improvement. A single successful seed establishes feasibility, not reliability. If feasible, compare several identical seeds and check timestep convergence around contact before promoting a fixed-gain configuration.

## Three directly useful papers

1. **Bogdanovic, Khadiv, Righetti — Learning Variable Impedance Control for Contact Sensitive Tasks** (2020 revision). Closest conceptual match: a policy outputs joint desired positions and impedance; damping scales with the square root of stiffness. The authors compare against tuned fixed-gain baselines, test contact uncertainty, and demonstrate hopper hardware transfer. Their discussion identifies simulation bandwidth/model mismatch and desired-position/gain ambiguity; gains suitable in one loading regime need not suit another. Read Sections II, IV-B/C, and VI. **Implication here:** establish a credible, numerically resolved fixed-impedance baseline first; later compare learned modulation against that baseline, not against an unstable high-gain straw man. Their gains and damping coefficient are platform-specific, not Z1 calibration values. The paper's trajectory-tracking regularizer is not the same thing as this repository's phase-indexed scripted-reference prior, nor authorization to add a reward now. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.07500v2).

2. **Tan, Liu, Turk — Stable Proportional-Derivative Controllers** (2011). Derives a discrete-time PD formulation that accounts for next-step state rather than simply applying conventional current-state PD at arbitrarily high gains. It is useful for understanding why continuous-time gain intuition does not establish numerical stability at a particular simulation timestep. **Boundary:** SPD is a different controller formulation; its stability result is not a theorem for the current force-clipped native affine actuator, MuJoCo's implicitfast implementation, or the constrained Z1 model. Read its formulation as a diagnostic reference, not a recommendation to replace the actuator. Saturation-specific evidence for this case comes from version-pinned simulator code, below. [Author-hosted paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf).

3. **Martín-Martín et al. — Variable Impedance Control in End-Effector Space: An Action Space for Reinforcement Learning in Contact-Rich Tasks** (IROS 2019). Compares action spaces across free motion, door opening, and wiping, including multiple fixed Cartesian impedance choices and policy-controlled impedance; reports simulation and real-robot transfer. **Implication here:** task interaction and baseline tuning matter when evaluating action spaces. **Boundary:** Cartesian impedance with its associated control structure is not equivalent to per-joint native stiffness via `set_gains`; wiping/door results do not establish hammer-impact impulse safety. This is supporting action-space evidence, not a reason to switch the thesis to Cartesian control. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08880), [authors' project and comparisons](https://stanfordvl.github.io/vices/).

## Calibration and numerical facts to keep separate

- The softer joint-6 numbers correspond to documented Z1 SDK defaults after conversion, not published permissible or safe gain bounds: SDK kp6=10 and kd6=2000; official bridge scales by 25.6 and 0.0128. The full vector need not be changed merely because one wrist diagnostic uses its default. [Official Z1 header](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/z1_sdk/blob/master/include/unitree_arm_sdk/message/LowlevelCmd.h#L33-L41), [official scaling implementation](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/z1_controller/blob/master/sim/IOROS.cpp#L45-L53).
- Warp v3.8.1 sets an actuator's velocity derivative to zero at its force bounds. Native implicit damping therefore does not guarantee a saturated servo is numerically benign. Applied force and timestep convergence remain the relevant evidence; do not raise torque limits or armature merely to hide the symptom. [Pinned implementation](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_warp/blob/v3.8.1/mujoco_warp/_src/derivative.py#L101-L107).
- This fixed-gain diagnostic is consistent with the agreed sequence: complete the fixed-impedance soft-CaT/impulse-constrained Z1 baseline, then add learned variable impedance. None of these papers proves that a softer constant wrist is optimal, that learned VIC will help this hammer task, or that a sim-only gain is hardware-safe. Those remain distinct empirical questions.
